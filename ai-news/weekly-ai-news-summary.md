## Research Highlights (arXiv cs.AI)

Note: arXiv's rss.arxiv.org feed only exposes the current day's new-submission listing (Sep 22, 2026 — 214 papers), not a rolling week, so these highlights reflect a single day's cross-section rather than the full week. Picks below were curated from that list for broad relevance, skipping narrow single-domain papers (medical imaging, niche robotics, etc.) unless clearly notable.

- **[Detecting Hallucination in LLMs: Tracing the Topological Signatures of Impaired Context Sharing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21096)** — Analyzing the topology (Forman-Ricci curvature) of attention-graph information flow reliably distinguishes hallucinated from non-hallucinated responses across multiple LLMs and benchmarks.
- **[World Modeling in Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21748)** — Using a toy Manhattan-navigation transformer, the authors show apparent "no world model" failures are actually caused by interference between superposed internal representations, not an absent world model.
- **[Self-Reference in Large Language Models: The Introspection Threshold for Recursive Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04277)** — Argues, via an analogy to von Neumann's self-reproduction threshold, that sustainable recursive self-improvement requires genuine introspection that current LLMs only partially exhibit.
- **[Rethinking Multi-Agent Collaboration: When More Is Less](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19759)** — Multi-agent setups only beat single-agent systems on long-horizon, loosely-coupled tasks; the authors' SAIGE mechanism shows adding more agents doesn't reliably improve outcomes.
- **[How do LLMs Compute Verbal Confidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17839)** — Mechanistic interpretability work finds models cache a genuine self-evaluation of answer quality right after generating an answer, rather than computing confidence scores on the fly or reciting token probabilities.
- **[Micro-Collaborative Poisoning: A Distributed Attack on RAG Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21573)** — A new RAG poisoning attack spreads a false claim across many individually-plausible documents, evading single-passage poisoning detection across 108 tested configurations.
- **[Intent-Governed Tool Authorization for AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22916)** — Proposes a server-side layer that narrows an agent's static tool permissions down to short-lived, request-specific "intent certificates" and checks proposed effects before execution.
- **[Large Language Models As Shannon Lossy Compressors Not Solomonoff Induction Estimators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05280)** — Argues standard next-token training objectives cannot implement Solomonoff induction and that reaching AGI-style recursive self-improvement likely requires neurosymbolic model synthesis, not just more scale.
